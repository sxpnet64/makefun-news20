## The day's stories

1. **Akvan comes ashore on India's east coast** — Cyclone Akvan began its landfall early on Friday, crossing the Andhra Pradesh–Odisha coast near Gopalpur after weakening from a severe cyclonic storm; winds 70–80 km/h gusting to 90. About 300,000 people were moved to safety in Odisha; 41 trains cancelled, 29 rescheduled. Sources: IMD via ETV Bharat, The Hindu, 20 Nov.
2. **Three leaders, one declaration — and no peace deal** — Trump, Xi and Putin met for 81 minutes in Shenzhen and issued a four-point "peace declaration" (UN reform, energy flows, courts, nuclear risk) with no mention of Ukraine or Iran. Putin then ruled out an energy or Black Sea truce after Ukraine's third strike on the Crimea bridge this week. Sources: Reuters, Dawn, The Independent, 19–20 Nov.
3. **Trump fires his cyber chief after a run of outages** — National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross was fired after outages hit an FAA ground stop, Microsoft Outlook, United Airlines, banks and an NSA network; Kirsten Davies (Pentagon CIO) takes over in an acting capacity. Sources: CNN, AFP, Washington Examiner, 19–20 Nov.
4. **Wall Street rout deepens as Dow loses 1,101 points** — Dow −2.19% to 49,097, first close below 50,000 since June; S&P 500 −2.02%, sixth straight loss; Nasdaq −2.25%. Anthropic's Nasdaq debut closed 17% above its $83 IPO price. Sources: Newspim, NDTV Profit, 19–20 Nov.
5. **Nikkei dives 4.16% as the sell-off spreads** — Nikkei −4.16%, Kospi −3.71%, ASX −1.13%; Japan's October inflation rose to 2.3%, its highest in a year. Source: NDTV Profit, 20 Nov.
6. **US cleared nearly 900,000 AI chips for China** — The Trump administration approved shipments of almost 900,000 advanced Nvidia and AMD AI chips to Chinese firms from December 2025 to May 2026, the House Foreign Affairs Committee said. Sources: The Standard, New Straits Times, 20 Nov.
7. **Houthis strike four targets across Saudi Arabia** — Strikes hit a Riyadh financial district (the capital's tallest skyscraper, the sovereign wealth fund HQ, damaged, no casualties), the main airport, a Jubail petrochemical plant and an air base; foreign investors warned to leave. Sources: TASS via Stockbiz, The Standard, 19–20 Nov.
